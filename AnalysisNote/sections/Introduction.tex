\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Achieving Standard Model predictions at the precision required for future collider programs, including the High-Luminosity LHC, demands QCD calculations with uncertainty control matched to percent-level experimental constraints. In this regime, theoretical uncertainty becomes a limiting factor, and precision QCD is central to robust interpretation of potential anomalies. Recent AI-for-science studies highlight both the opportunity and the evaluation burden of this regime, where scientific utility depends on rigorous validation rather than purely generative capability~\cite{Wang:2023aai,Messeri:2024aiu}.

Precision QCD can be pursued at hadron colliders, but tests are most cleanly controlled in lepton-collider environments with well-defined initial states. Although there is currently no active high-energy lepton collider, archived LEP data now provide an unprecedented open-data opportunity for precision event-shape studies through sustained curation and reanalysis efforts~\cite{EEAlliance:website,Badea:2019vey,Chen:2021uws}. Relative to hadron- and nuclear-collision open-data settings, the LEP $e^{+}e^{-}$ environment provides substantially tighter control of soft-event complexity and initial-state structure, making it especially powerful for precision comparisons between experiment and theory.

Event-shape observables measured at LEP continue to anchor comparisons between perturbative calculations, generator modeling, and non-perturbative hadronization constraints. Among these observables, thrust is a primary precision test because it probes both perturbative radiation and non-perturbative dynamics in a single distribution~\cite{PhysRevLett.39.1587,Abbate:2010xh,Heister:2003aj}. Recent thrust-based extractions report $\alpha_s(m_Z)\simeq 0.1136\pm 0.0012$, below the 2023 world-average value $\alpha_s(m_Z)=0.1180\pm 0.0009$~\cite{Benitez:2024nav,ParticleDataGroup:2022pth}. This persistent tension, often referred to as the $e^{+}e^{-}$ $\alpha_s$ puzzle, sharpens the need for renewed measurements with explicit uncertainty control and publishable covariance information. The thrust peak and logarithmic moments are also increasingly important for constraining non-perturbative shape-function effects~\cite{Abbate:2010xh,Assi:2025ibi}.

This analysis is part of a broader AI-for-science program where precision is non-negotiable. AI systems are increasingly used to assist theoretical physics calculations and model construction~\cite{Carleo:2019mlphys,Karagiorgi:2022mlnewphysics,Cranmer:2023lqcdml,Gukov:2024rigorml}, while agent-oriented scientific workflows are developing rapidly in both autonomous laboratory settings and LLM-based discovery frameworks~\cite{Abolhasani:2023sdl,MacLeod:2020sdl,Chen:2024scienceagentbench,Lu:2024aiscientist}. Recent AI+scientist studies emphasize recurring workflow patterns, including problem decomposition, iterative refinement with tool feedback, and adversarial-style verification loops, while also showing that domain-expert validation remains the limiting requirement for scientific reliability~\cite{Woodruff:2026practicalsi,Bubeck:2025coscientist}. In high-energy theory, early case studies now include AI-assisted amplitude and event-shape calculations~\cite{OpenAITheory:2026singleminus,Schwartz:2026cparameterllm}. Physics utility, however, requires direct confrontation with experimental data. The curated LEP open-data environment enables a concrete theory--experiment loop in which AI-assisted calculations, observables, or model variants can be validated against high-precision measurements under domain-expert control.

Within that program, the immediate objective of this note is to establish a robust detector-corrected thrust baseline using Iterative Bayesian Unfolding (IBU) implemented in RooUnfold, with complete validation and systematic propagation in a workflow that can be iterated rapidly. In this stage, thrust is unfolded and validated end-to-end, including covariance and correlation products intended for downstream phenomenology use. In the next stage, the same framework will be extended to additional thrust-family observables (C-parameter and heavy-jet mass), and multidimensional unfolding requirements will be assessed.

A central part of this note is the reconstruction-level validation of the particle inputs that enter thrust. Since thrust is computed from selected charged and neutral particles, the modeling of particle-category kinematics directly impacts migration, regularization behavior, and final unfolded uncertainties. For this reason, the note includes detailed \texttt{pwflag}-resolved kinematic comparisons between data and simulation before the unfolding stage.
