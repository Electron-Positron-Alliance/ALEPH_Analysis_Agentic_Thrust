\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Figure~\ref{fig:final_thrust_result} shows the unfolded spectrum with the total uncertainty band and comparison to the published ALEPH 2004 result~\cite{Heister:2003aj}. Agreement is good across the bulk of the spectrum ($0.62\le T<0.99$), with the largest discrepancy in the highest thrust bin. This is physically expected: the dijet limit is sensitive to MC modeling of soft hadrons, and the hadronic correction
$C_{\mathrm{had}}(j)$ and ISR correction $C_{\mathrm{ISR}}(j)$ carry growing uncertainties there. Potential dataset differences between the current analysis and the ALEPH 2004 result can also contribute at the endpoint.

The per-component correlation matrices are derived from the individual component covariance matrices $\mathrm{Cov}^{(c)}_{ij}$ described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:stat_covariance}. For each component $c$, the correlation is 
\begin{equation}
\rho^{(c)}_{ij} = \frac{\mathrm{Cov}^{(c)}_{ij}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Cov}^{(c)}_{ii}\,\mathrm{Cov}^{(c)}_{jj}}}.
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:corr_matrices_per_source} shows all seven components simultaneously, enabling direct comparison of their correlation structures. The components differ mainly in correlation length, and the ordering follows directly from whether the underlying variation is bin-local or coherent across the spectrum. The data statistical component exhibits the narrow correlation band characteristic of IBU: neighboring bins are positively correlated through the unfolding propagation, but the correlation falls off quickly with bin separation. The response matrix MC statistical component behaves the same way, since both originate in bin-local Poisson fluctuations spread over neighboring bins by the unfolding. The ISR correction is nearly diagonal, consistent with its sub-percent size and with the bin-local character of the MC statistical uncertainty on the $C_{\mathrm{ISR}}(j)$ ratio, and the hadronic correction is modestly correlated, commensurate with its ${\sim}2\%$ magnitude in the central range. The iteration component is somewhat broader, as the regularization responds coherently to low-frequency features of the spectrum rather than to individual bins. Broader still is the experimental component, driven by the neutral-energy response shifts (\texttt{nes\_up}, \texttt{nes\_down}), which displace the spectrum in the
same direction over a wide range of bins. The theory component has the strongest and most extended off-diagonal correlations: generator reweighting alters the parton-shower and hadronization description, and therefore the overall spectral shape, rather than any localized
region. It is the dominant source of off-diagonal structure.
